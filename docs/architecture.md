# Architecture

`Mod/RuntimeBrain.cs` is the single authoritative brain implementation. The game and runtime tests compile that same physical source. `Mod/script.cs` registers the Human variation and creates a brain only after the game-specific asset loader validates the payload. Integration files handle native sensing, actuation, lifecycle and diagnostics; no alternate/demo graph is substituted.

The shared immutable asset is a thresholded MaleCNS v1.0 derivative: 176,422 neurons and 6,287,749 connections retained at weight ≥5, excluding self-edges. The PNG carries the exact compressed FLYB bytes. Runtime verifies compressed SHA-256, dataset/version, counts, CSR monotonicity, targets and used metadata indexes. Each person has independent dynamic neural state. The scalar reader reuses an eight-byte buffer instead of allocating an array for every neuron/edge scalar; variable-length reads still own their arrays and truncated reads still fail.

Each control tick reconciles tracked limbs and samples a fresh bounded frame. Native measurements are normalized; validity is tracked for health, oxygen, consciousness, circulation, damage, vitality and blood deficit. Missing/nonfinite readings do not become positive deficit signals. Damage uses valid connected-limb health samples only, with a separate explicit-loss denominator for limb loss. Vitality may fall back to valid connected-limb health. Known detached limbs remain excluded from body sensing and actuation while their liquid identities remain available to diagnostics; they are also excluded from own collision, nearby and audio signals. Foot-floor impacts are still contact, including self-generated movement.

Vision is a nearest-collider line-of-sight/ambient-light proxy. Audio is active external physical-object playback with own-source and Root/Root filtering. Vibration and legacy body proprioception are derived mechanical channels. Connected native hinge angles/speeds supply the new joint encoders; signed anchor velocity and tilt are separately labeled. Falling reads downward native limb velocity with floor-contact gating. Ambient temperature comes from the native grid; nearby dynamic objects and lava add attenuated thermal cues. Static geometry does not create cold signals. Acid-pool exposure requires contact at the status anchor (within 0.05 world units), a limited proxy that can miss acid touching another limb. None of these channels establishes semantic perception or a biological sensory model.

Connected, readable limb health is sampled against its previous valid baseline. The largest positive normalized drop becomes one DamageEvent; persistent damage is a separate diagnostic. Changes to InitialHealth, invalid/lost/disconnected samples, suspension and terminal state clear/re-prime history. A fatal limb transition can emit once while the person remains nonterminal. The injury encoder uses a saturating Hill-derived gain, explicitly separate from native Pain. Regional contact maps recognized human roles to disjoint fly bristle/tactile groups using existing nerve annotations; broad impact and injury merge by maximum rather than stimulating a neuron twice. See the exact formulas and anatomical limitations in sensory-mapping.md.

The nearest visible collider supplies a 2D bounds-radius and rigidbody-relative-motion feature estimate, never a rendered retinal image. One reusable 512-bin spectrum buffer samples only the strongest accepted external AudioSource once per read. Squared non-DC amplitudes give below/above-100 Hz energy fractions scaled by current playback strength. Invalid/empty spectra retain the broad playback proxy; last-heard history is never input. This is Unity source analysis with startup history and coarse resolution, not biological hearing or guaranteed low latency.

Liquid sensing enumerates each tracked circulation's `LiquidDistribution` every control tick. Finite positive amounts are normalized by a finite positive `TotalLiquidAmount`; invalid entries contribute no exposure. A cleared, reused dictionary retains the largest current per-ID fraction across tracked limbs for the Senses page, never a cumulative dose or stale history. Exact case-sensitive IDs from the inspected stock registry select engineered exposure routes. Unregistered display names are text only. Non-blood liquids, including unknown and unusual serums, populate `LiquidExposure` telemetry. They no longer stimulate an invented neural receptor route. Recognized sedative/stimulant/restorative categories retain existing engineered decoder adjustments; native effects are observed separately. `LiquidWater` is reserved and zero for stock circulation because no plain water ID is registered. `Infection` is the legacy internal field for native `IsZombie`, now labeled `zombie(native)`; it is not inferred from a reanimation agent. Reading liquids does not inject them or enact their native syringe effects. See the exact coverage in api-compatibility.md.

The sensory router drives real populations selected by MaleCNS type/class/subclass/superclass/nerve metadata. See [sensory-mapping.md](sensory-mapping.md) for exact selectors, directions, reference sources and intentional unmapped fields. It no longer paints internal injury/chemistry onto photoreceptors or directly stimulates locomotor descending neurons. All eligible members are considered, replacing a biased first-N input cutoff. Sparse LIF processing caps each tick at 24,000 processed neurons, prioritizes current sensory input and discards stale overload work. `STEADY`/`OVERLOADED` describe this scheduler; they are not a measured real-time factor. The cap limits processed neurons, not outgoing edges or wall-clock cost. Currently refractory neurons do not consume processing slots; post-fire cleanup removes only the fired IDs from next-tick queues; propagation skips targets still refractory on the arrival tick. Inputs on the exact recovery tick remain eligible. This frees budget without changing refractory duration; overloaded firing patterns can change because eligible neurons receive the freed slots.

`ManualInputState` is session-local state owned by each controller. `RuntimeBrain` resolves it after the native frame has been encoded and immediately before the existing population injection path. Mixed mode replaces only selected routes; Manual only zeroes unselected external routes without clearing recurrent state, potentials, refractory timers or pending synaptic work. The resolver also owns finite normalized values, world-horizontal direction, bounded neural-tick pulses and latest live/effective readings. Manual feature routes are visited even when their natural observations are absent. The original `SensoryFrame` remains authoritative for telemetry, lifecycle, movement permission and decoder constraints.

Motor decoding starts with latest-tick fired fractions in the reference walking, backward, halt and brake populations, plus side-specific leg motor subclasses. Forward/backward and leg channels use 150 ms exponential filters; lateral DNa02/DNg13/DNa01 activity uses a 100 ms filter and maps to head/core joint requests, not 3D facing. A forward mode enters at filtered 0.08 and exits below 0.04 after a 250 ms minimum dwell. Raw MDN fraction >=0.5 preempts forward with backward mode, which exits below filtered 0.25 after its minimum dwell. Active modes use a 0.3 neural walk floor; the adapter applies a 0.55 final walk floor after modifiers so default gain 2 clears the native 0.5 walking gate with decay margin. These are engineering decoder settings, not neural rates or guaranteed gait. MN9 proboscis activity is not human grasping; brain grip requests are zero and therefore neutral while active. Hazard does not directly choose an escape direction and water does not start an independent oscillator. Neural halt/brake clears requests immediately; other motor/grip requests are rate limited to eight normalized units per elapsed game second, with at most 0.25 seconds used by each smoothing step. Terminal/invalid health and unavailable/low consciousness clear motor output immediately; terminal state also clears neural state, while invalid health preserves neural state for later recovery. Nonterminal unconsciousness can still be sampled neurally. Restorative requests remain engineered, sensory-derived interventions, not proven neural healing.

The adapter submits walking, joint and optional grip requests through native APIs with local limb eligibility. Explicit conversion defaults are a walking gain of 2 (then -1..1 clamping) and a 30 deg/s full-scale joint target; the component exposes bounded overrides. These are engineering choices, not native-game calibration. Retained last-heard audio text and its real-time timestamp are display history only; they never contribute to sensory sound input. **Native locomotion mechanics are still involved.** Inspected `PersonBehaviour.Update` decays walking requests; `LateUpdate` selects a pose. The controller's `[DefaultExecutionOrder(-1000)]` refreshes its cached permitted walking intent after that decay and before the inspected default-order native `LateUpdate`; full neural application remains tick-rate controlled. `LimbBehaviour.ManagedFixedUpdate` can run `ApplyPoseOverrides`, `MoveIntoPose` and `FakeStandUpright`; the latter applies native torque using `FakeUprightForce`. Suppressing menu buttons does not disable those paths. No claim of exclusive neural standing/balance control is made, and this polish does not change native pose/torque semantics.

The controller preserves fractional tick time, so a 20 Hz request works over 50 Hz physics callbacks. At most one neural step runs per callback; excess whole intervals are dropped without catch-up. Actual elapsed game time is passed to motor smoothing. Diagnostics record control-loop milliseconds, input age in unscaled time and dropped game time. Simulation time follows game callbacks, not continuous biological time.

`BrainVisualization.cs` retains every finite soma position with its original neuron index and superclass category. The full graph remains simulated. The map data is shared with the immutable asset; there is no per-frame graph scan. The screen display projects raw X/Z coordinates with a common scale, labels the projection without inventing anatomical orientation, and marks actual fired IDs in a captured tick. All positions are rasterized into a 256 x 320 texture, so nearby points can share pixels and zoom enlarges the existing raster. It normally uploads that texture at most ten times per second of unscaled UI time (direct resizing can refresh it sooner) while the Brain page is selected, and only for a new captured tick. Map buffers are released on deselection or leaving the Brain page. Formatted UI text and render snapshots are cached at this refresh interval. Whole-graph spike history records every processed tick, including ticks between captures; population bars show captured fired/member counts, not Hz.

The Canvas/TextMeshPro display is one opaque top-center screen overlay for a selectable person. The pure TelemetryLayout helper bounds its scale and dimensions, reserves side space for game controls and keeps collapse anchored in place. A separate auto-hiding scrollbar shows reading position without covering content. The title bar drags the panel; the lower-right handle resizes it down to 360 by 300 logical units. Position is clamped to keep the header and handle reachable. Size and position are session-only and shared across the selected-person panel. It does not inherit world position, camera orientation, scene depth or lighting. Overview, Senses and Brain pages wrap text and scroll; text-size controls and collapse preserve screen space. A stable session number and world coordinates identify the selected person. The controller owns registration and texture disposal. Disable invalidates the adapter sample and clears display history/timing; re-enable waits for a fresh tick. Ordinary person selection preserves history. No game persistence is implemented. Offline doubles and source checks do not replace native UI/physics/loading/performance acceptance.
